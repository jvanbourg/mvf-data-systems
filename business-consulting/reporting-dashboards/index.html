Reporting & Business Intelligence

Management reports and dashboards that arrive on time, agree with each other, and answer the questions leadership actually asks.

Most reporting problems aren’t dashboard problems. They come from unclear metric definitions, data that doesn’t reconcile between systems, and reports rebuilt by hand every month. Good business intelligence fixes those foundations first, then automates what’s left.

Discuss Your Reporting All business services

Signs your reporting needs work

  • Month-end management reports take weeks to produce.
  • Two reports show different numbers for the same thing.
  • Profitability by product, customer, channel or location is guesswork.
  • One person holds the spreadsheet everything depends on.
  • Dashboards exist, but people still export to Excel to check them.

What good looks like

A short list of agreed metrics with written definitions. Data pulled automatically from the systems of record, checked against each other, and modeled once. Reports and dashboards built on that model, owned by named people, and documented well enough that the business isn’t dependent on any one person, including the consultant who built them.

Approach

  1. Define the questions

    Agree which decisions the reporting must support and write clear definitions for each metric.

  2. Trace the data

    Identify where each figure comes from, how reliable it is, and where systems disagree.

  3. Model & automate

    Build a clean reporting data model and automate extraction, validation and refresh.

  4. Deliver & hand over

    Reports and dashboards your team can use, with documentation and training for ownership.

Typical deliverables

  • KPI and metric definitions agreed with leadership
  • Data source map and reconciliation checks
  • Reporting data model or data mart
  • Automated management reports
  • Management dashboards (for example in Power BI)
  • Documentation and handover to your team

Tools and experience

Marc works with SQL, Python and Power BI (DAX, Power Query), and relational databases including SQL Server, Oracle and MySQL. Where a business already has a reporting tool, the first step is usually making better use of it.

His reporting background includes analytic budgeting, worldwide financial reporting and performance-management systems for public institutions and large companies, as well as data warehouse models and indicators for multi-site healthcare platforms.
