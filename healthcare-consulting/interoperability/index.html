Healthcare Data Integration & Interoperability

Plan clinical data integration so it is well scoped, standards-based and delivers usable data.

Integration projects fail less often on technology than on scope: unclear use cases, data that means different things in different systems, and vendors left to interpret requirements. Marc helps healthcare organizations and health-tech companies define what must be exchanged, how it should be modeled, and how to get there in phases.

Discuss an Integration All healthcare services

What Marc helps with

Integration planning

Use cases, data flows, source and target systems, HL7 v2 messages or FHIR resources, and a phased roadmap that delivers value early.

Clinical information modeling

Data models and minimum datasets for clinical, care-coordination and research data, including semantic reconciliation across specialties.

Terminology & normalization

Mapping local codes to SNOMED CT, LOINC, ICD-10/ICD-11 and ATC, and normalizing source data before integration or migration.

Data governance

Ownership, master data, data quality rules and shared-management arrangements across departments, sites or partner organizations.

Vendor & technology assessment

Independent review of integration proposals, platforms and vendor capabilities against your requirements.

Delivery oversight

Client-side oversight of interface and platform vendors through build, testing and acceptance.

Relevant experience

  • Multi-hospital BI on high-cost cancer drugs (Ministry of Health / ATIH): source analysis, data normalization and interoperability linking the EHRs of five hospitals to a national monitoring system.
  • National biobank catalog (INSERM): structural and semantic analysis, national and European interoperability review, minimum-dataset data model.
  • Hospital–community care coordination (Agence eSanté, Luxembourg): requirements aligned with the national shared patient record and interoperability services.
  • Regional shared oncology record: hospital database assessment (modeling, quality, MDM, interoperability) and normalization ahead of migration.
  • Real-time therapeutic monitoring (Dijon University Hospital): data-flow and interoperability architecture.
